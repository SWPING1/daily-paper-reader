## Abstract 

Emotion Recognition in Conversation (ERC) has become a fundamental capa-bility for large language models (LLMs) in human-centric interaction. Beyond accurate recognition, recent studies highlight the importance of coherent emo-tional expression, yet both abilities are severely limited by the scarcity and static nature of high-quality annotated data.In this work, we propose SELF-EMO ,a self-evolution framework grounded in a psychologically motivated hypoth-esis: the better a model predicts others’ emotions, the better it can generate its own emotionally consistent responses . Build-ing on this insight, we explicitly incorporate two auxiliary tasks— emotional understanding and emotional expression —and formulate a role-based self-play paradigm in which the model simultaneously acts as an emotion recognizer and a dialogue responder. Through iterative interaction, the agents continuously generate diverse conversational trajectories, forming a scalable data generation process. To ensure training quality, we construct a data flywheel mechanism: each self-play rollout produces multiple candidate emotional predictions and responses, which are then filtered via a smoothed IOU-based reward to select high-quality samples. The selected samples are fed back into training, enabling continuous self-improvement without external supervision. Based on this paradigm, we fur-ther develop SELF-GRPO , a reinforcement learning algorithm that stabilizes optimization under diverse emotional outputs by combining multi-label alignment rewards with group-level consistency signals.Extensive experiments on three bench-mark datasets (IEMOCAP, MELD, and EmoryNLP) demonstrate that SELF-EMO achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance without relying on external retrieval or auxiliary models. Under a unified training setting, our method improves average accuracy by +6.33% on Qwen3-4B and +8.54% on Qwen3-8B, with consistent gains across all benchmarks. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed self-evolution paradigm and highlight its strong generalization ability.

2.1 ERC Methods 

Prior work in the field of ERC has primarily concentrated on three methodological paradigms: Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) [ 9], Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) [ 10 ], and Pre-trained Language Models (PLMs) [ 11 ]. In RNN-based studies, DialogueRNN [ 12 ] and DialogueCRN [ 13 ]adopt RNN architectures to track contextual information and the individual states of speakers throughout the conversation. In GNN-based studies, models capture relational information by constructing graph structures among utterances and speakers. For example, DialogueGCN [ 14 ] treats utterances as graph nodes and builds edges based on contextual information, while DAG-ERC [ 15 ]further incorporates speaker identity and utterance position into the graph. In studies based on pre-trained language models, BERT-ERC [ 16 ] leverages models such as BERT and improves performance by introducing a fine-grained emotion classification module and a two-stage training strategy. With the rise of LLMs, current research in the ERC field mostly uses LLMs combined with retrieval-augmented methods to improve model performance on the ERC task, among which InstructERC [ 6]is the first study to introduce these methods in this field. Subsequently, BiosERC [ 8] further adds character traits to the input and adopts LoRA [ 17 ] training to efficiently fine-tune the model. Further-more, PRC-Emo [ 7] proposes to additionally include internal and external emotional descriptions of speakers in the input and employs a curriculum learning strategy to enhance model performance. However, these works introduce static knowledge bases for retrieval and incorporate external knowl-edge analysis, which also lead to problems of poor model generalization ability and high inference resource consumption. 

2.2 Self-Play for Self-Evolution 

Due to the rise of LLM-based methods, an increasing number of works have begun to focus on how to achieve a closed training loop solely through data generated by the model itself under the constraint of limited high-quality human-crafted data [ 18 , 19 , 20 ], thereby significantly reducing the cost of manual data creation. This approach has yielded substantial benefits in multiple domains. For instance, AbsoluteZero [ 21 ] was proposed in the code generation domain, training the model itself as a user instruction generator to obtain high-quality problems while simultaneously training its ability to solve the generated problems. In the mathematical reasoning domain, works such as SVS [ 22 ]and PromptCot [ 23 ] have also demonstrated that the model itself can serve as a high-quality problem generator, producing problems of appropriate difficulty for the model to perform reinforcement learning and improve problem-solving performance. However, self-play works all need to address a fundamental issue: how to design a logically rigorous operational paradigm for self-play to achieve a closed training loop. 

2.3 Psychological Connections of Emotion Tasks 

At present, there have already been a large number of psychological studies that have made detailed arguments and experiments on the connection among the three tasks of emotion understanding and recognition, self-emotion generation, and expression. Among them, the theory of levels of emotional awareness [24 ] indicates that individuals must first recognize, differentiate, and label emotions before they can conduct more complex emotional reasoning and social emotion regulation [ 25 ], and the differentiation of others’ emotions is the core of explaining others’ behaviors and generating appropriate responses. On the contrary, existing clinical evidence [ 26 ] shows that the lack of emotional differentiation ability will directly impair empathy and expression. These studies all indicate that emotion understanding and recognition is the premise of self-emotion generation and expression. Moreover, studies [ 27 ] show that others’ emotions will influence the observer’s emotions and behavioral decisions, and the occurrence of this phenomenon can be summarized as that how one understands others’ emotions will directly control self-emotion generation and expression.

3.1 Semi-supervised Multi-task Entropy Reduction 

We introduce two unsupervised auxiliary tasks to support the ERC task To: self-personality understanding (Ts) and self-emotion expression (Tr ). Given a dialogue context C and a personality trait P I , the model produces three outputs sequentially: 

To = πθ (eo | C, P I ), Ts = πθ (es | C, P I, e o), Tr = πθ (r | C, P I, e o, e s) (1) where eo is the predicted emotion of the interlocutor, es is the model’s self-perceived emotional response, and r is the self-emotion expression generated by Tr .

Theoretical Derivation Drawing on cognitive appraisal theory, an agent’s internal state is shaped by its appraisal of others’ emotions. We model this as: 

Ts = fs(To, C, P I ) + ϵs, Tr = fr (To, T s, C, P I ) + ϵr (2) where fs and fr are the appraisal and expression mappings, and ϵs, ϵr are residual terms. From an information-theoretic perspective [ 28 ], optimizing To via reinforcement learning reduces the output entropy Hπθ (eo | C, P I ). Given the functional dependencies above, this propagates to the auxiliary tasks: 

↓ Hπθ (eo | C, P I ) ⇒ ↓ Hπθ (es | C, P I, e o) ⇒ ↓ Hπθ (r | C, P I, e o, e s) (3) This shows that supervised training on To implicitly reduces uncertainty in Ts and Tr , providing a theoretical basis for the unsupervised components in SELF-EMO.

## 4 Experiments 

This section introduces the experimental setup, the experimental results, and an analysis of the results. 

4.1 Experimental Setting Datasets. Our methodology is grounded in three widely recognized benchmarks in the ERC do-main: IEMOCAP [30 ], MELD [ 31 ], and EmoryNLP [32 ]. These datasets serve as the seed data for persona initialization. Specifically, IEMOCAP consists of dyadic conversations among ten speakers (5,163/647/1,623 samples for train/dev/test). MELD and EmoryNLP, both derived from television series with multi-party dialogue settings, comprise 9,989/1,109/2,610 and 7,551/954/984 6utterances, respectively. All model evaluations are strictly performed on the official test splits of these benchmarks. 

Baselines. To verify the effectiveness of our approach, we compare it against prominent ERC methods, categorized into two groups: (1) Traditional methods , including DialogueRNN [ 12 ], ICON [ 33 ], DialogueGCN [ 14 ], COSMIC [ 34 ], MMGCN [ 35 ], DAG-ERC [ 15 ], LR-GCN [ 36 ], Multi-DAG+CL [ 37 ], CBERL [ 38 ], DER-GCN [ 39 ], and LSDGNN+ICL [ 40 ]; (2) LLM-based methods , such as InstructERC [6], BiosERC [8], and PRC-EMO [7]. 

Implementation Details. The experiments are conducted on a cluster of eight NVIDIA H100 GPUs, utilizing Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B [ 2] as the primary backbone models. Departing from the common practice where models are restricted to single-dataset training—a setup prone to overfitting and limited generalization—this work adopts a more robust strategy. Specifically, a single unified model is trained across all three ERC training sets and subsequently evaluated on each official test split. For a rigorous and fair comparison, the two most competitive LLM-based baselines are retrained under this identical multi-dataset setting. Additional hyperparameter configurations and training details are provided in Appendix E. 

4.2 Main results 

As shown in Table 1, under the unified training setting, existing LLM-based methods exhibit limited generalization across different conversation contexts. In contrast, the proposed SELF-EMO frame-work achieves substantial performance gains. Specifically, our model realizes an accuracy increase of +6.33% on the Qwen3-4B backbone and +8.54% on the Qwen3-8B backbone, with consistent improvements observed across all benchmarks. These results demonstrate the robust generalization of SELF-EMO and empirically validate the effectiveness of the self-evolution paradigm in enhancing emotional intelligence through autonomous agent interactions. 

4.3 Ablation Experiments 

We conduct ablation experiments on SELF-EMO-4B to assess each component’s contribution (Table 2). Regarding the data produced by SELF-EMO, w/ COLD uses the cold-start data generated by the expert model to first perform SFT, and then uses the resulting model as the base model for the RL training stage. w/o SELF-GRPO indicates that we directly apply the vanilla GRPO algorithm for RL-stage fine-tuning. w/o SELF-EMO indicates that we train with GRPO using all data from the original datasets. The experiments demonstrate that our method does not rely on data produced by the expert model. Instead, during the cold-start phase, the model only learns the corresponding reasoning and output patterns, and incorporating expert-generated data for SFT actually degrades model performance. SELF-GRPO enables the model to perform macro-level reward modeling over sampled outputs, leading to more stable emotional understanding outputs and consequently improved performance. Overall, both the self-play data generated by SELF-EMO and the training algorithm are crucial to the model’s performance gains; applying GRPO fine-tuning with only the original data leads to a sharp average performance drop of 5.18%.

## 5 Conclusion 

In conclusion, we present SELF-EMO, a psychologically motivated reinforcement learning framework that leverages three strongly correlated tasks for collaborative entropy reduction. SELF-EMO achieves state-of-the-art ERC performance under the Dataset-Unified Setting while significantly reducing inference overhead, and the data produced during training helps alleviate the scarcity of high-quality annotations in this domain.
