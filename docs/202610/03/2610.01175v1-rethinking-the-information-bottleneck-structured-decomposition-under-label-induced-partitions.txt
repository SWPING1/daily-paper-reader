# Rethinking the Information Bottleneck: Structured Decomposition under Label-Induced Partitions

School of Computer Science, The University of Sydney Sydney, NSW 2006, Australia 

## Abstract 

Standard information bottleneck (IB) regularization constrains representations via a single scalar I(Z; X), implicitly treating all information as homogeneous. However, a single global compression control couples label-relevant structure with residual within-condition variation, rather than regulating their allocation indepen-dently, allowing nuisance information to persist in learned representations. For example, in medical imaging applications, residual variation often stems from acquisition conditions, background factors, or subject-specific appearance. This issue becomes particularly pronounced in data-limited settings, where models tend to overfit such variation, hindering generalization. While existing regularization methods can stabilize training, control capacity, or shape representation geometry, they do not explicitly separate nuisance-like variation from task-supporting struc-ture. To address this limitation, we revisit IB from a structured perspective based on a label-induced partition, where condition-level structure and within-condition information play distinct roles. This leads to a dual-bottleneck formulation: a stan-dard KL term controls global information capacity, while a conditional KL term targets within-condition information. We show that the conditional KL admits an exact decomposition into a within-condition information term and a prior-mismatch term, explaining its alignment with the design objective. With a simplex-structured conditional prior, the method provides controllable latent geometry and integrates seamlessly into existing pipelines. Experiments on classification and segmentation show the clearest gains in low-data classification and consistent improvements across dense prediction benchmarks. 

## 1 Introduction 

Learning latent representations with strong generalization is a central goal in supervised learning. Not all encoded information should be treated equally: some components support label-induced separability, while others merely reflect residual within-condition variation that is easy to memorize but unreliable for prediction. This is especially salient in medical imaging, where acquisition condi-tions, background factors, and subject-specific appearance contribute substantial residual variation. In this work, we formalize this distinction under a label-induced condition variable C = π(Y ),where Y denotes the class label and π maps labels to condition groups, and study how to preserve condition-level structure while more directly constraining within-condition information. Among principled approaches, the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle and its variational formu-lation, the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB), view regularization as balancing predictive relevance against compression [1, 2, 3]. In standard VIB, this is implemented by constraining 

I(Z; X). However, under the label-induced partition, the total information admits 

I(Z; X) = I(Z; C) + I(Z; X | C).

This reveals a regularizer-level structural coupling: a single global compression control jointly penalizes condition-level structure and within-condition variation, leaving their allocation without

an independent control axis. Encoders with the same global budget may therefore retain different amounts of residual information, a limitation formalized in Sec. 4.2. This issue is especially relevant in medical imaging, where acquisition and subject-specific variation can be substantial, but it also applies more broadly when residual variation is easy to memorize yet weakly aligned with label-induced separability [4]; see Sec. 3.1. We therefore introduce a dual-bottleneck objective, where a standard KL term controls global capacity and a conditional KL term targets within-condition information under C = π(Y ).To instantiate this realization in a structured way, we further introduce a class-conditional prior con-strained by regular-simplex geometry. This design provides a symmetric and controllable geometric scaffold for the conditional prior, injects a separability-oriented inductive bias into the latent space, and reduces direct reliance on empirical prototype construction at initialization. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We present a structured information-control view under a label-induced partition, showing that global compression controls the aggregate information budget but not its allocation between condition-level and within-condition information. • We propose a dual-bottleneck objective as a direct operational realization of this view, and derive an exact decomposition of the conditional KL term into a within-condition information component and a condition-wise prior-mismatch component—a closure property that makes the conditional KL a principled surrogate for constraining I(Z; X | C).• We establish a task-constrained two-axis upper-bound result: the dual regularizer simulta-neously upper-bounds visible within-condition nuisance dependence (through Jcond ) and conditional-invisible condition-level encoding (through Jstd ), neither of which is controlled by the other surrogate alone; the corresponding family-inclusion property follows as an immediate corollary. • We introduce a simplex-constrained conditional prior with fixed shared variance for practical instantiation, and experiments on supervised classification and segmentation, including medical-image benchmarks, demonstrate consistent improvements over the unregularized baseline, with the clearest gains in data-limited regimes. 

## 2 Related work 

Information bottleneck and variational formulations. The Information Bottleneck (IB) princi-ple formalizes supervised representation learning as a trade-off between predictive relevance and compression [1]. This view was later extended to deep models [2] and made practical through variational objectives such as the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) [3]. However, a single global bottleneck on I(Z; X) can be too coarse for deep classification, especially in deterministic or continuous-input regimes [5]. Related work also suggests that a single principle may be insufficient in complex representation-learning settings [6]. Our work addresses a different limitation: under a label-induced partition, the global budget conflates condition-level structure with within-condition variation, motivating a dual bottleneck. 

Conditional bottlenecks and conditional priors. Our conditional term is related to prior condi-tional information-control objectives, including conditional VIB-style formulations for multi-view representation learning [7], conditional compression objectives for self-supervised visual represen-tations [8], and recent conditional information-bottleneck formulations for sequence modeling and time-series imputation [9, 10]. These works show that conditioning the bottleneck can be useful, but their conditions arise from views or augmentations rather than from a supervised label-induced partition. In contrast, our condition variable is C = π(Y ), which is used to separate condition-level structure I(Z; C) from within-condition information I(Z; X | C). Moreover, we retain both Jstd and 

Jcond , so global capacity control is not replaced by conditional control. Condition-dependent priors also appear in CVAEs and semi-supervised generative models [11, 12], where they serve generative likelihoods or ELBOs. Here, the conditional prior instead acts as a supervised information-control prior whose KL term decomposes into I(Z; X | C) plus condition-wise prior mismatch, with simplex geometry providing a symmetric instantiation. 

Representation geometry and class structure. A complementary line of work improves su-pervised representations by shaping their geometry. Center loss, prototype learning, supervised 2contrastive learning, and MCR 2 promote intra-class compactness, inter-class separation, or class-wise compressibility through empirical centers, prototypes, pairwise objectives, or coding-rate criteria [13, 14, 15, 16]. In contrast, we impose geometric structure directly at the conditional prior, and ground it in an information-theoretic decomposition that enables targeted control of within-condition information without empirical centers. Simplex geometry and neural collapse. Neural Collapse studies show that deep classifiers often approach highly symmetric Simplex ETF configurations, and that such geometry can be optimal under simplified feature models [17, 18]. ## 3 Problem description, basic assumptions, and design goal 

3.1 Residual variation as a representation concern 

In this work, we treat regularization not as generic capacity control alone, but as representation-level selection of what to preserve and what to suppress. Classical regularizers mainly control capacity or stabilize optimization [19, 20, 21, 22], while information-bottleneck methods make this selection explicit through compression-oriented objectives [1, 3]. This concern is especially salient in medical-image set-tings, where acquisition conditions, background factors, and subject-specific appearance can vary substantially. At the same time, the underlying issue is not domain-specific: in supervised representation learning more broadly, residual within-condition variation can coexist with task-supporting structure and therefore requires ex-plicit control. This view is consistent with prior information-theoretic analysis. Under the assumptions that N is a nuisance for Y (i.e., I(N ; Y ) = 0 ), Z is sufficient for Y so that 

I(Z; Y ) = I(X; Y ), and N → X → Z, Achille and Soatto [23] derive 

I(Z; N ) ≤ I(Z; X) − I(X; Y ).

Equivalently, under sufficiency this can be read as 

I(Z; N ) ≤ I(Z; X) − I(Z; Y ). We use this result only as motivation: information retained beyond what is needed for the task can carry nuisance dependence, so residual variation is a meaningful object of control. Thus, retaining more input information than the task strictly requires does not guarantee that nuisance-related dependence vanishes. Fig. 1 provides dataset-level evidence on BloodMNIST: some low-level statistics vary strongly across samples but separate labels weakly. Although this analysis is not an information-plane estimate, it supports treating residual variation as a distinct object of control. 

3.2 Information categories under a label-induced partition 

We now formalize the above concern in a standard supervised representation learning setting. Let 

X ∈ X denote the observed input, Y ∈ Y the supervised target, and Z ∈ Rd the latent representation 3produced by a stochastic encoder qθ (z | x). We further introduce a finite condition variable induced by the supervised target, C = π(Y ), where π maps labels to condition groups. This condition variable is not intended to exhaust all task semantics; rather, it serves as an analytical coordinate for distinguishing different informational roles in the learned representation. Under this coordinate system, we distinguish two categories of information. The first is condition-level structure I(Z; C), namely the component aligned with the label-induced partition and supportive of separability across conditions. The second is within-condition information I(Z; X | C), namely the dependence that remains between the representation and the input after conditioning on the partition. The former is the structure we aim to preserve relatively, while the latter is the component we aim to constrain more directly. Accordingly, the design goal is to realize a more selective form of representation control: 

I(Z; X) = I(Z; C) + I(Z; X | C).

This decomposition follows from the chain rule and the encoder-induced independence Z ⊥ C | X;see Appendix A.1–A.2 (Theorem 2). We use it to separate condition-level from within-condition information. 

3.3 Assumptions and interpretation scope 

We conduct the analysis in a clean supervised learning setting. For interpretation, we assume that the observed input can be written as 

X = T (S, N ), Y = f (S), (1) where S denotes the semantic pattern supporting the supervised target, N denotes additional pertur-bation or background variation, and T combines them into the observed input. This setup allows us to focus on input complexity and residual variation, rather than confounding effects from noisy or corrupted labels. Let C = π(Y ) denote the label-induced condition variable introduced above. We treat labels as reliable so that the decomposition and conditional modelling are well defined, and use C as the coordinate for separating condition-level structure from within-condition information. While medical imaging provides a motivating regime with substantial residual variation, the formulation is domain-agnostic and applies broadly to supervised representation learning settings where residual variation coexists with task-relevant structure. These assumptions are introduced for interpretability; the proposed method does not depend on them in practice. Minimally, I(Z; X | C) is the residual dependence between Z and X after conditioning on C. A stronger nuisance-based interpretation requires additional conditional-independence structure and is introduced later as a complementary view rather than as a prerequisite for the method; see Sec. 4.2 and Appendix A.5 (Proposition 1). 

## 4 Theory: information-control view 

4.1 Label-induced information coordinates 

We analyze representation control under C = π(Y ), introduced in Sec. 3.2, as a coordinate for two informational roles. Since the posterior encoder is qθ (z | x), Z ⊥ C | X; Theorem 2 gives 

I(Z; X) = I(Z; C) + I(Z; X | C).

The first term captures condition-level structure aligned with the label-induced partition, while the second term captures residual dependence on the input after conditioning on that partition. Thus, the central control question is not merely how much information Z retains from X, but how the total information budget is allocated between condition-level structure and within-condition residual variation. 4.2 Why single-axis control is insufficient 

Even when C = Y , where the exact IB objective can be rewritten in these coordinates, its single com-pression coefficient couples the reward on I(Z; Y ) with the penalty on I(Z; X | Y ). More generally, a global-only bottleneck penalizes the aggregate I(Z; X), but does not provide an independent axis for regulating its split between I(Z; C) and I(Z; X | C). Indeed, Appendix A.3 constructs encoders with identical I(Z; X) but different I(Z; X | C) (Theorems 3–4). Therefore, global information control alone is insufficient for selectively suppressing within-condition residual variation. Conversely, conditional control alone is also incomplete. It can target within-condition dependence, but it does not by itself constrain global or condition-level encoding. Appendix A.7 gives a structural regime in which a condition-level component B is invisible to I(Z; X | C), while still occupying positive global information capacity (Proposition 3). This shows that a purely conditional bottleneck can miss information aligned with the condition variable, making a global control axis still necessary. The within-condition term also admits an optional nuisance interpretation. Suppose that 

X = T (S, N ), Y = f (S), C = π(Y ),

where S denotes task-relevant signal and N denotes within-condition nuisance variation. If, condi-tioned on C, the variables satisfy the Markov chain 

N → X → Z, 

then the conditional data-processing inequality gives 

I(Z; N | C) ≤ I(Z; X | C).

Thus, when residual variation can be modeled as N , controlling I(Z; X | C) also controls a visible nuisance-dependence axis. This nuisance reading is optional: I(Z; X | C) is not nuisance information in general, but it upper-bounds nuisance dependence under the stated conditional Markov assumption. A formal statement is provided in Appendix A.5 (Proposition 1). Taken together, the theory identifies two complementary residual-information axes: a visible within-condition nuisance axis, controlled through I(Z; X | C), and a conditional-invisible condition-level axis, controlled through global information capacity. This motivates a dual control view rather than a single-axis bottleneck, as illustrated schematically in Fig. 2(b)–(d) and visualized in latent space in Fig. 3.

## 6 Experiments

Under the same backbone, data split, optimizer, schedule, and task loss, we compare NO REG , S TD KL, C OND KL, and D UAL KL, isolating global control, conditional within-condition control, and their combination. ## 7 Conclusion, limitation, and future work 

We introduced a structured information-control view of bottleneck regularization under label-induced partitions. Unlike standard IB/VIB objectives that constrain a single global information quantity, our formulation separates global capacity control from within-condition residual control. The main theorem shows that this dual regularizer simultaneously upper-bounds two structurally distinct residual-information axes — visible within-condition nuisance dependence and conditional-invisible condition-level encoding — neither of which is controlled by either single-term bottleneck alone. Operationally, we instantiate this view through KL upper bounds, a simplex-structured conditional prior, and a shared-variance parameterization, yielding a regularizer that can be integrated into existing supervised learning pipelines. Across classification and segmentation benchmarks, the method shows consistent improvements over the unregularized baseline, with the strongest gains in low-data classification and stable improvements across dense prediction benchmarks. The proposed method is best viewed as a representation-level regularizer for settings where label-relevant structure coexists with residual within-condition variation, consistent with prior analyses showing that the benefit of information constraints depends on the data-generating structure [31, 32]. Its empirical benefit can depend on the model family, optimization, task loss, and whether within-condition variation is residual rather than task-relevant; gains may be smaller when classes are already highly separable or intra-class variation itself carries task semantics. Beyond medical imaging, related settings such as PCB defect inspection may also benefit when similar residual variation is present. Future work may explore richer conditional-prior families, more flexible geometries, and heterogeneous or condition-dependent residual structures. 9References

11 A Detailed proofs for the label-induced conditional bottleneck 

This appendix provides detailed derivations for the mathematical claims used in the main method. Our goal is not only to state the final objective, but also to make explicit: (i) which statements are exact identities, (ii) which statements are upper bounds, (iii) which statements are existence/insufficiency results, and (iv) which parts specify practical geometric or initialization choices used to instantiate the regularizer. 

A.1 Notation and proof roadmap Standing regularity assumptions. Throughout this note, we work with stochastic encoder families 

qθ (z | x). We assume that the KL divergences and mutual informations appearing below are well-defined and finite, and that the relevant aggregated posteriors ¯qθ (z) and ¯qθ (z | c) exist. In the continuous setting, this excludes degenerate deterministic limits of the encoder for the purpose of the stated identities and bounds. Let X ∈ X be the input, Y ∈ Y the supervised target, and Z ∈ Rd the stochastic latent variable generated by the encoder qθ (z | x).We use a label-induced finite condition random variable 

C = π(Y ),

where 

π : Y → C , C = {1, . . . , K },

is a finite partition map. In the clean supervised interpretation used in Sec. 3.3, one may write 

Y = y⋆(X), and hence C = π(y⋆(X)) . This deterministic clean-label case is a concrete instance of our setting, but the Markov-based decomposition in Theorem 2 only requires the encoder-induced conditional independence stated below. 

Encoder-induced Markov convention. All information quantities involving Z are evaluated under the joint law induced by the data distribution and the stochastic encoder. For any upstream variable 

W that is not directly provided as an encoder input, 

pθ (w, x, z ) = p(w, x )qθ (z | x),

and hence 

pθ (z | x, w ) = qθ (z | x) = pθ (z | x).

Thus W → X → Z forms a Markov chain. We use this convention with W = C in Theorem 2, with

Proof roadmap. The proof chain is organized into four layers according to their role in the paper. 1. Core justification. Supporting calibration results. These results clarify how the two KL terms relate to their corresponding information quantities under variational parameterization: • Lemma 1: exact decomposition of the standard KL term. • Proposition 2: tightness statements under exact prior calibration. 3. Optional structural interpretations. A.2 Exact decomposition under a label-induced condition Theorem 2 (Exact decomposition under encoder-induced Markovity) . Suppose that the representation is generated from the input through the stochastic encoder qθ (z | x), so that C → X → Z forms a Markov chain as in Appendix A.1. Then 

I(Z; X) = I(Z; C) + I(Z; X | C).

Proof. Start from the chain rule for mutual information: 

I(Z; X, C ) = I(Z; C) + I(Z; X | C).

On the other hand, the chain rule also gives 

I(Z; X, C ) = I(Z; X) + I(Z; C | X).

By the encoder-induced Markov chain C → X → Z,

I(Z; C | X) = 0 .

Hence I(Z; X, C ) = I(Z; X). Combining the two identities yields 

I(Z; X) = I(Z; C) + I(Z; X | C).

This proves the claim. In the clean supervised setting described in Appendix A.1 and Sec. 3.3, 

C = π(y⋆(X)) is a sufficient concrete instance of this Markov condition, but determinism of C is not required for the decomposition.

A.3 Why a single global bottleneck is insufficient Theorem 3. There exist two encoders q1(z | x) and q2(z | x) such that 

Iq1 (Z; X) = Iq2 (Z; X), Iq1 (Z; X | C)̸ = Iq2 (Z; X | C).

Hence, any regularizer depending only on I(Z; X) cannot, in general, separately control within-condition information. Proof. Let C ∼ Bernoulli(1 /2) and N ∼ Bernoulli(1 /2) be independent random variables. Define the input 

X = ( C, N ).

Now define two deterministic encoders: 

Z1 = C, Z2 = N. 

Since Z1 is a deterministic one-bit function of X,

I(Z1; X) = H(Z1) − H(Z1 | X) = H(Z1) = 1 .

Similarly, since Z2 is also a deterministic one-bit function of X,

I(Z2; X) = H(Z2) − H(Z2 | X) = H(Z2) = 1 .

13 Therefore, 

I(Z1; X) = I(Z2; X).

We next compare the conditional mutual informations. For Z1 = C, once we condition on C, the latent variable Z1 is fully determined. Hence 

H(Z1 | C) = 0 , H(Z1 | X, C ) = 0 ,

and therefore 

I(Z1; X | C) = H(Z1 | C) − H(Z1 | X, C ) = 0 .

For Z2 = N , independence of N and C gives 

H(Z2 | C) = H(N | C) = H(N ) = 1 .

Moreover, since Z2 = N is determined by X = ( C, N ),

H(Z2 | X, C ) = 0 .

Thus 

I(Z2; X | C) = H(Z2 | C) − H(Z2 | X, C ) = 1 .

We have shown 

I(Z1; X) = I(Z2; X) = 1 , I(Z1; X | C) = 0 , I(Z2; X | C) = 1 .

Hence, two encoders can have the same global information budget but different within-condition information. Therefore, a regularizer depending only on I(Z; X) cannot, in general, distinguish them. 

Scope. The theorem establishes that a condition-aware term is necessary for separately controlling within-condition variation under the stated design goal. It does not imply that the conditional KL term is the unique possible realization of such a term; that point is addressed by Theorem 5.

This subsection clarifies the sense in which the proposed dual-bottleneck objective admits a loss-agnostic information-theoretic interpretation. A central observation is that the two KL terms play consistent representational roles across supervised objectives: the standard KL regularizes global information capacity, whereas the conditional KL regularizes within-condition information relative to the label-induced partition. 

Proposition 6 (Regularizer-level universality) . Consider any supervised objective with target Y , and suppose: (i) there exists a finite label-induced partition C = π(Y ); (ii) the encoder/prior families are such that the displayed KL terms are well-defined; (iii) the aggregated posteriors ¯qθ (z) and ¯qθ (z | c)

exist; (iv) the task loss ℓtask (gω (z), y ) is well-defined. Then the dual-bottleneck objective is a well-defined regularized supervised objective. Proof. Well-definedness follows directly from assumptions (i)–(iv): a finite label-induced partition 

C = π(Y ), well-defined KL terms, existing aggregated posteriors, and a well-defined task loss together ensures that each term in the dual-bottleneck objective is properly defined. For the regularizer-level loss-agnostic interpretation, apply the decomposition identities for the two KL terms. By Lemma 1, the unconditional KL controls Iθ (Z; X) up to a global prior mismatch; by Theorem 5, the conditional KL controls Iθ (Z; X | C) up to a condition-wise prior mismatch. These identities depend on the encoder and prior families rather than on the specific form of ℓtask .Therefore, the representational roles of the two KL regularizers are independent of the supervised loss choice at the regularizer level. 

Remark on loss dependence. The information-theoretic interpretation above concerns the reg-ularizing action imposed on the encoded representation. The eventual task-level behaviour of a learned model is jointly determined by the regularizer, the predictive loss, the model family, and the optimization setting. In particular, a well-defined supervised loss can still be task-agnostic; for example, 

ℓtask (g(z), y ) ≡ 0,

or more generally ℓtask (g(z), y ) = h(g(z)) , which is independent of y. Such losses satisfy well-definedness but provide no task-discriminative learning signal. Hence, this proposition is a regularizer-level universality statement, not a task-performance invariance guarantee. 

Interpretation. These results characterize the proposed method as a loss-agnostic representation regularizer in the following precise sense: the two KL terms preserve stable information-theoretic roles across supervised objectives, thereby defining a structured mutual-information-based mechanism for constraining encoded features. This interpretation is centered on how the regularizer shapes the learned representation through complementary control of global capacity and within-condition variability.

## C Literature review 

Relation to conditional bottlenecks and conditional priors. Our formulation is related to prior uses of variational bottlenecks, conditional information quantities, and conditional latent priors. Deep VIB [3] provides a variational KL surrogate for global information bottleneck regularization, and our standard term Jstd follows this global-capacity role. However, our motivation is different: under a label-induced condition variable C = π(Y ), the global budget I(Z; X) decomposes into condition-level structure I(Z; C) and within-condition information I(Z; X | C), so a single global bottleneck does not specify how the retained information is allocated. Conditional bottleneck-style objectives have also appeared in top-venue representation-learning work. Hwang et al. [7] derive conditional VIB-style terms from a total-correlation objective for multi-view representation learning, where the conditions correspond to observed views rather than supervised label-induced partitions. Lee et al. [8] incorporate conditional information bottleneck-style compression into SimCLR and BYOL to improve self-supervised visual representations and robustness. These works show that conditional information control is a meaningful design principle, but their objectives target multi-view calibration or self-supervised compression. In contrast, our condition variable is induced by supervised labels, and the conditional KL is used to control within-condition information in a supervised task representation. Our use of a condition-dependent prior is also distinct from conditional generative models such as CVAEs [11] and semi-supervised deep generative models [12]. In those models, conditional latent variables or priors are part of a generative likelihood or ELBO. In our method, the conditional prior is not introduced for generative modelling; it is a variational information-control prior inside a supervised regularizer. Thus, the novelty is not the existence of a conditional prior alone, but the supervised dual-bottleneck construction: Jstd controls global capacity, Jcond controls within-condition information through an exact decomposition, and the simplex conditional prior provides a symmetric and controllable geometric instantiation. 41 Table 15: Comparison with related bottleneck and conditional-prior formulations.

Our method keeps this global control through Jstd , but adds a label-induced conditional control axis Jcond motivated by I(Z; X) = 

I(Z; C) + I(Z; X | C). Thus, our objective separates global capacity control from within-condition information control. Multi-view conditional VIB-style objectives [7] These objectives use conditional information terms to calibrate shared representations across multiple observed views and to han-dle view imbalance or missing views. Our conditioning variable is not a view but a supervised label-induced partition C = π(Y ),and our goal is not multi-view calibration but supervised regular-ization of within-condition residual information. Self-supervised compressive visual representations [8] This line of work uses conditional information bottleneck-style objectives to compress self-supervised representations, typically between augmented views. Our method uses a supervised condi-tion variable C = π(Y ) and combines conditional information control with an explicit global KL term. Hence, the method is a su-pervised dual-bottleneck regularizer rather than a self-supervised compression objective. Conditional VAE / deep con-ditional generative models [11] Conditional VAEs use condition-dependent latent variables or pri-ors inside a generative likelihood or ELBO for structured output modelling. plus condition-wise prior mismatch. Semi-supervised deep gener-ative models [12] Semi-supervised generative models use class variables and latent variables to model data density and exploit unlabeled data.
